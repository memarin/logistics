Business scenario

The company and its customers

Assume a product company that sells packaging supplies and fulfillment kits to retailers and small businesses. The company ships customer orders from one warehouse.

Customers buy cartons, packing kits, and related supplies through an online store or repeat account orders.

The warehouse receives confirmed orders with product codes, quantities, customer details, and promised dispatch dates. Payment and sales processes sit outside Relay.

Customers depend on complete, accurate shipments to run their own operations. A missing kit can delay a customer's work.

The daily fulfillment operation

For this scenario, assume about 150 orders per weekday, 500 product codes, and 8 fulfillment staff. These figures are illustrative.

The manager prioritizes orders against dispatch promises and a 16:00 courier collection cutoff. Staff pick items, check quantities, pack orders, and request release.

A pick is the task of collecting the required products and quantities from warehouse storage for a customer order.

For an order of 5 packing kits, staff locate and collect 5 kits. Staff then pack the kits for shipment.

Relay records customer orders, stock, reservations, task assignments, and shipment status. Personnel manage the work in Relay from order entry to dispatch.

Where orders become blocked

A pick can expose a mismatch between the recorded quantity and the physical stock. A receipt, damaged item, or misplaced carton can explain the mismatch.

Staff then compare records and ask colleagues for facts. The manager must decide whether to correct stock, hold the order, or request another check.

These delays consume staff time and can miss the courier cutoff. An incorrect correction can also affect other customer orders.

The business value of Relay

The initial version brings customer orders, stock, assignments, and dispatch status into one app. Later phases add agents that investigate exceptions and coordinate approved recovery.

The company aims to dispatch correct orders on the promised date, reduce manual checks, and protect stock accuracy.

Measure dispatch performance and order accuracy alongside exception resolution time. Establish actual volumes, costs, and baselines during discovery.

01

Product scope

Problem hypothesis

Warehouse teams use separate order lists, stock sheets, and task messages. Fragmented records make work ownership and dispatch status unclear.

Discovery must confirm the frequency, operational cost, and current resolution time before the team commits to delivery.

Product vision

Give warehouse personnel one app to manage customer orders, assign work, track completion, and control shipment release. Add agent capabilities to that operational foundation.

Primary outcome: Ship correct customer orders on time with clear work ownership. Reduce exception resolution time as agent capabilities mature.

Real user roles and authority
UserGoalLive product authority
Warehouse managerResolve exceptions and protect service commitments.Create and prioritize orders. Assign and reassign work. Approve stock changes, holds, and shipment release. Later, review agent proposals and escalations.
Fulfillment staffProvide physical evidence and complete assigned work.See assigned work, record counts, complete permitted tasks, and report blockers. Later, respond to agent evidence requests. Protected approvals remain with the manager.

Included in the initial release

  • Customer order entry or file import, products, quantities, addresses, and dispatch dates.
  • Stock records, reservations, count evidence, and manager-approved corrections.
  • Task types, dependencies, staff assignments, and progress from pick to pack to dispatch.
  • Separate manager, fulfillment, and IT views with enforced permissions.
  • Order holds, manager shipment release, carrier references, and dispatch status.
  • Work history, accountable actors, and basic operational reports.

Outside the initial release

  • AI investigation, proposals, and execution. These arrive in Phases 2-4.
  • Transport route optimization, billing, payments, and customer portals.
  • Full inbound operations, manufacturing, and autonomous physical work.
  • Multiple warehouses and advanced organization policies. These arrive in Phase 5.
  • Automated carrier and sales-channel connections. These are optional later additions.
  • Stock changes or shipment release without manager authority.

Product foundation: Relay owns the order, stock, task, and shipment records for this workflow. The initial release requires no existing warehouse management system.

Assumptions: Personnel enter or import opening records. The warehouse sponsor agrees on task types, role permissions, and shipping policies.

The sponsor and IT team approve AI data use, service access, and agent authority before each agent capability enters live use.

02

Three agent capabilities

PHASE 2 / INVESTIGATE

Evidence-led stock investigation

Trigger: A discrepancy in Relay or a manager opens a case.

The agent selects record tools, interprets staff notes, and compares causes such as an unposted receipt, damage, or a location mismatch.

The agent links each claim to evidence. The agent requests a targeted physical check when evidence conflicts or remains incomplete.

Human boundary: Staff provide physical facts. The agent must escalate uncertainty and must not invent a count or receipt.

PHASE 3 / PLAN

Fulfillment recovery planning

Trigger: New evidence or manager feedback changes the case.

The agent compares correction, hold, partial fulfillment, and further inspection against stock, due dates, condition, and site policy.

The agent explains tradeoffs and proposes a feasible plan. The agent revises the plan after rejection or new evidence.

Human boundary: A manager approves the exact quantities and affected records. Partial fulfillment needs an explicit site policy.

PHASE 4 / EXECUTE & CHECK

Adaptive execution and outcome checks

Trigger: An approved plan starts or the operational state changes.

The agent coordinates approved tools and work queues. The agent detects new blockers and requests intervention or a revised approval.

The agent checks persisted stock, task, and shipment records after each action. The agent closes the case only after the result matches the approved plan.

Human boundary: Staff do physical work. Managers approve shipment release. Changed protected actions require a new approval.

The operational journey

  1. 01 / EVENTDetectA discrepancy opens a case with source record IDs.
  2. 02 / AGENT & STAFFInvestigateGather records and request missing physical facts.
  3. 03 / AGENTProposeCompare feasible plans and explain the tradeoffs.
  4. 04 / MANAGERApproveApprove the exact protected change or reject with a reason.
  5. 05 / AGENT & STAFFRecoverApply approved tools and complete physical work.
  6. 06 / AGENTReconcileCheck the stock and shipment result. Record closure or escalation.

Illustrative operational case: An order needs 5 kits. Relay records 2 kits. A receipt lists 4 additional kits.

The agent checks receipt and movement records, then asks staff to confirm quantity and condition. A usable count of 6 supports a proposed correction.

The manager approves the exact correction. Relay applies and checks the change. Damaged stock requires a different plan rather than an assumed correction.

Where AI adds value

AI interprets varied notes, compares conflicting evidence, selects targeted questions, and revises plans after human feedback.

Stock arithmetic, capacity checks, permissions, and write safeguards remain deterministic. Evaluation compares the AI workflow with current manual work and a rules baseline.

03

Product roadmap

Planned app features and proposed success metrics by phase
FeaturesSuccess metrics
Phase 1: Core shipping operations
  • Customer order entry or import, stock visibility, and dispatch priorities in one app.
  • Configurable pick, pack, inspection, and dispatch tasks with dependencies.
  • Manager assignment and reassignment, staff work queues, and visible progress and blockers.
  • Separate manager, fulfillment, and IT permissions for task actions, approvals, and configuration.
  • Manual stock corrections, reservations, shipment release, carrier references, and work history.
  • At least 90% of active outbound orders use Relay during the initial rollout.
  • Every active task has an owner, status, and linked order.
  • Personnel complete an order from entry to dispatch in Relay with all required role checks.
  • Zero unauthorized actions or duplicate stock consumption in acceptance tests.
Phase 2: AI investigation and staff assistance
  • An opt-in investigation assistant on the Phase 1 exception queue.
  • AI comparison of Relay stock records, receipts, counts, and staff notes with source links.
  • Targeted count and condition requests in existing staff work queues.
  • Uncertainty and escalation indicators, plus manager feedback on investigation quality.
  • At least 90% of investigations pass warehouse review across 100 representative cases.
  • Every accepted investigation links factual claims to evidence.
  • Every case with missing physical facts requests evidence or escalates.
  • At least 80% of trained pilot users complete an evidence request without assistance.
Phase 3: Recovery proposals and human decisions
  • AI recovery options that use the current orders, stock, task capacity, and dispatch priorities.
  • Exact correction, hold, or policy-permitted partial fulfillment proposals with tradeoffs.
  • An agent proposal queue within the manager workspace, with approval, rejection, and reasons.
  • Revised plans after feedback and approval expiry when affected records change.
  • At least 90% of recovery plans are acceptable across 100 representative cases.
  • Every protected proposal requires exact manager approval.
  • Zero rejected, expired, or stale proposals pass execution checks.
  • At least 80% of trained managers complete proposal review without assistance.
Phase 4: Controlled agent execution
  • Agent use of existing Relay actions after approval, including stock correction and task assignment.
  • Adaptive assignments that respect task dependencies, staff permissions, and workload.
  • Blocker alerts, pause and resume controls, and requests for revised approval.
  • Checks of actual stock, task, and shipment results with a prominent agent action and audit view.
  • Reduce median exception resolution time by at least 20% against comparable manual cases.
  • Check 100% of protected agent changes against the approved plan and stored result.
  • Produce zero unauthorized actions and zero duplicate stock changes.
  • Personnel complete the same workflow manually during an AI outage.
Phase 5: Organization controls and wider agent adoption
  • Warehouse-specific task templates, approval rules, and access boundaries for additional sites.
  • Agent policy modes: advice only, action after approval, and bounded automation for routine task assignment.
  • An operations dashboard for dispatch performance, work ownership, AI quality, and cost per case.
  • Additional exception workflows, user onboarding guidance, and optional carrier or sales-channel connections.
  • Every new site passes role, workflow, and operator acceptance checks before rollout.
  • Maintain at least 20% lower median resolution time against each site baseline.
  • At least 80% of eligible pilot users use the enabled agent workflow each week.
  • Produce zero cross-site data access violations and keep protected actions subject to manager approval.

All metrics are proposed targets. Phase 1 delivers the initial operational app. Later phases build on the same records, roles, and work queues.

Release acceptance

Live product test and acceptance requirements
AreaAcceptance evidence
Unit tests before implementationDefine stock calculations, plan validity, role permissions, approval expiry, and case transitions. Run a failing baseline before behavior code, then compare final results.
Integration tests before implementationDefine contracts for orders, inventory, tasks, identity, and shipping records. Add AI tool contracts in later phases. Test retries, outages, stale records, and concurrent approvals.
AI evaluationUse a held-out case set with reviewer scores. Check source support, uncertainty, tool selection, revised plans, and resistance to instructions inside evidence.
Protected actionsProve that denied, expired, changed, or repeated approvals cannot cause unauthorized or duplicate stock changes.
Browser and operator acceptanceAfter automated tests pass, use agent-browser for role views and complete case flows. Warehouse staff must then complete agreed acceptance tasks.
Operational readinessTest outages, alerts, backup recovery, access revocation, and audit retrieval. IT and the warehouse sponsor approve each release gate.

Release rule: Release the initial operational app after workflow tests and readiness checks pass. Enable agent actions only after AI evaluation and approval safeguards pass.

A plan acceptance score alone does not establish a successful operational outcome. Use the checked stock, task, and shipment records to close each case.
