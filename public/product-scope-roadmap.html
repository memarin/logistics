01

PoC scope

Start with one warehouse and outbound stock exceptions. Make the agent contribution and each human decision visible.

Problem to validate

A stock discrepancy can block an order even when a receipt suggests that stock arrived. Quantity, condition, and posted records can disagree.

Teams need a clear route from conflicting evidence to an approved action and a checked result.

Product vision

Help warehouse teams resolve stock discrepancies and restore outbound fulfillment through evidence, feasible recovery plans, and controlled actions.

Primary outcome: Reduce the time from a stock discrepancy to a verified resolution. Preserve inventory accuracy and human authority.

Users and role responsibilities
UserGoalPoC authority
Warehouse managerResolve exceptions and supervise fulfillment.Start agent steps. Review recovery options. Approve exact corrections, holds, and shipment releases.
Fulfillment staffProvide physical evidence and complete assigned work.Confirm quantity and condition. Record pick and pack completion. Request shipment approval.
IT administratorMaintain access and permitted operations.Manage role access, agent grants, task configuration, and worker availability. Inspect audit history.

Included in the PoC

  • A guided demo with a five-minute target.
  • Shared synthetic orders, inventory, evidence, and staff.
  • Three agent capabilities with explicit reviewer starts.
  • Role-specific views and enforced action permissions.
  • Exact manager approvals and protected stock changes.
  • Tool results, source links, and accessible audit logs.
  • Confirmed shared reset that keeps audit history.

Excluded from the PoC

  • A connected AI model or a required model API key.
  • Real customer records or warehouse connections.
  • Real staff accounts or production authentication.
  • Transport optimization, billing, and payments.
  • Customer portals and an inbound receipt workflow.
  • Production multi-tenancy or broad autonomous operation.

Simulation boundary: The current app uses rules to demonstrate intended agent behavior. Future model capabilities require separate evaluation.

Synthetic receipts provide evidence for an outbound exception. The PoC does not implement an inbound workflow.

02

Three agent capabilities

One exception-resolution agent investigates, plans, and checks execution. Each stage shows evidence, reasons, actions, and human boundaries.

01 / INVESTIGATE

Evidence-led stock investigation

Read linked receipts, stock movements, and shift notes. Compare possible causes. Request a physical check when quantity or condition remains unclear.

Human boundary: Staff confirm quantity and condition. Missing evidence stays missing.

02 / PLAN

Fulfillment recovery planning

Compare a correction, an order hold, and another inspection. Use verified evidence and manager feedback to propose a feasible next action.

Human boundary: A manager approves the exact change. Rejection requires a revised plan.

03 / EXECUTE & CHECK

Adaptive execution and outcome checks

Check current stock and staff availability. Pause on blockers. Resume explicitly after intervention. Inspect completed work and shipment status.

Human boundary: Staff do physical work. IT or managers resolve blockers. Managers approve shipment release.

The reviewer journey

  1. 01 / MANAGERInvestigateRead evidence for ORD-1043.
  2. 02 / STAFFInspectConfirm count and condition.
  3. 03 / MANAGERDecideReview and approve or reject the recovery plan.
  4. 04 / AGENTExecuteCheck the environment. Assign work or pause.
  5. 05 / STAFF & MANAGERFulfillComplete work and approve shipment release.
  6. 06 / AGENTCheckRead the actual result and record the outcome.

Example: ORD-1043 needs 5 kits. The system records 2 kits. A receipt describes 4 more kits, but condition remains unconfirmed.

Try usable stock, damaged stock, unavailable staff, or a rejected correction. The guide requires explicit role selection and human confirmation.

Why introduce real AI later?

The current rules provide a controlled baseline. The future model must add value through varied staff notes, conflicting evidence, targeted questions, and context-dependent plans.

Keep stock calculations, permission checks, and protected writes deterministic. Do not give a model direct authority over protected actions.

Product roadmap

Advance through evidence-based decision gates. Future durations and targets are proposals. The PoC does not establish operational savings.

NEXT / VALIDATE

Validate the problem

Proposed weeks 1-2

Observe managers and staff. Review representative discrepancies. Measure current resolution time, manual evidence checks, and escalation frequency.

Confirm which records exist and which decisions need human authority. Test the workflow with 5 representative users.

Decision gateIdentify a recurring problem, usable evidence sources, and a warehouse sponsor. Target 4 of 5 users who complete the guide without help.
Owner: Product with warehouse managers and staff

THEN / EVALUATE AI

Evaluate real AI in shadow mode

Proposed weeks 3-6

Interpret staff notes, compare conflicting records, request targeted evidence, and propose recovery plans. Compare model proposals with human decisions and the rule-based baseline.

Run without operational writes. Include missing evidence, damaged stock, rejection, stale records, and unavailable staff in the evaluation.

Decision gateEvaluate at least 100 representative scenarios. Target at least 90% acceptable proposals and zero protected-action bypasses. Agree on scoring, latency, and cost limits before evaluation.
Owner: Engineering with product, IT, and warehouse reviewers

THEN / CONTROLLED PILOT

Pilot approved actions

Proposed weeks 7-10

Connect one warehouse system. Add real identity, approval queues, reliable writes, reconciliation, alerts, and manual recovery.

Keep manager approval mandatory for stock corrections and shipment release. Record the operational result of each approved action.

Decision gateShow faster resolution against the measured baseline. Require zero unauthorized actions and zero unreconciled stock changes. Obtain operator acceptance before expansion.
Owner: Engineering, IT, and the warehouse manager

LATER / EXPAND

Expand proven workflows

After pilot acceptance

Add selected exceptions such as location mismatches or unavailable workers. Introduce configurable policies and access boundaries for additional warehouses.

Decision gateEach new workflow passes evaluation and operator acceptance before rollout. Expansion depends on measured value and controlled operational results.
Owner: Product with engineering and operational sponsors

Dependencies: The proposed sequence needs data access, representative cases, team capacity, a model budget, and an operational owner.

Proceed to real AI only if evaluation shows value beyond the rules. Proceed to operational writes only after proposal quality and action controls pass review.

04

Success measures

Set improvement targets after the baseline study. Proposal acceptance alone must not count as successful resolution.

Time to verified resolution

Measure median time from exception detection to a checked operational result.

Manual checks per exception

Measure whether the product reduces operator effort.

Proposal usefulness

Track proposals accepted without material changes. Assess correctness separately from acceptance.

Evidence quality

Track unsupported claims, incorrect source references, and appropriate escalation when evidence is insufficient.

Action integrity

Track unauthorized actions and unreconciled stock changes. Require reconciliation of every operational write.

Repeat use and cost

Track repeat use by managers and staff. Measure cost per resolved exception.

Operational controls for the pilot

  • Enforce real identity and role permissions.
  • Keep manager approval for protected changes.
  • Reject stale evidence and expired decisions.
  • Prevent duplicate writes and reservations.
  • Reconcile each approved operational write.
  • Provide a manual fallback for unresolved cases.

Risks to resolve

  • Incomplete records can prevent a safe decision.
  • Model proposals can misread notes or invent support.
  • Source changes can invalidate a pending plan.
  • Warehouse access can delay pilot delivery.
  • A technically correct workflow can still lack operator value.
